# Visual direction research

Date: 2026-08-23

## Question

How can North feel more immediate and desirable to men around 18–22 without
turning into a generic red-and-black fitness, gambling, crypto, or “alpha male”
product?

This note informs a later visual-direction decision. It does not replace
`DESIGN.md` and does not authorize a theme change.

## What the evidence supports

- **More expression, not more decoration.** Google reports that Material 3
  Expressive was preferred by up to 87% of participants aged 18–24. Its use of
  color, size, shape, motion, and containment also helped participants find key
  actions up to four times faster. Google explicitly warns that unfamiliar or
  unstructured interaction patterns still reduce usability. North should make
  the question, selected answer, and next action more visually decisive while
  retaining familiar vertical scrolling, labelled controls, and clear
  containment. [Google Design research](https://design.google/library/expressive-material-design-google-research)
- **The demographic is visually fluent.** Canva's State of Visual
  Communication research says 91% of Gen Z respondents believe visuals
  communicate ideas better than text. This is workplace research rather than
  consumer-app research, so it is directional rather than conclusive, but it
  supports replacing long stretches of visually uniform prose with stronger
  composition and visual hierarchy. [Canva Gen Z research](https://www.canva.com/newsroom/news/gen-z-work-research/)
- **Current visual culture is moving away from frictionless corporate polish.**
  Canva's 2026 trend analysis used platform search behavior, billions of
  designs, and a survey of 1,000 creators. It found growth in raw, tactile,
  editorial, bold-type, zine, and deliberately imperfect visual language. The
  “bold typography” and related DIY search cluster rose 77% year over year.
  This is broad creator data, not young-men-only data, but it argues against
  making North merely cleaner or more professionally neutral.
  [Canva 2026 trends](https://www.canva.com/newsroom/news/design-trends-2026/)
- **Strong color is current, but red and black are not a demographic truth.**
  Canva's 2025 usage data lists black and white plus energetic red, blue, and
  yellow among its community's most-used colors. Pinterest's 2026 palette,
  derived from searches and saves from 600 million people, includes Cool Blue,
  Plum Noir, Wasabi, and Persimmon; Pinterest also reports Gen Z interest in
  discovering new aesthetics. These sources justify testing color-forward
  palettes, not claiming that a particular hue is inherently masculine.
  [Canva 2025 Design DNA](https://www.canva.com/newsroom/news/design-dna-2025/)
  [Pinterest Palette 2026](https://business.pinterest.com/pinterest-palette/)
- **Avoid borrowing the visual grammar of masculinity influencers.** Movember
  surveyed more than 3,000 men aged 16–25 in the US, UK, and Australia. Sixty-
  three percent regularly watched masculinity influencers and 43% of viewers
  found them motivating, but viewers also reported worse outcomes and 27%
  reported feelings of worthlessness. North can offer direction and momentum
  without echoing the status, domination, wealth, and body-pressure aesthetic
  of that content. [Movember research](https://us.movember.com/movember-institute/masculinities-report)
- **Young men should not be treated as one narrow masculine archetype.**
  Equimundo's 2025 UK study frames the opportunity as positive engagement,
  purpose, connection, and a broader model of masculinity; 91% of respondents
  named friendship as a defining masculine trait. A stronger North should feel
  challenging and alive, not hostile or shaming.
  [Equimundo research](https://www.equimundo.org/resources/state-of-uk-men-2025/)

## Direction 1: Rally editorial

The recommended direction. North becomes a piece of contemporary sports
editorial: urgent, physical, and composed, without pretending to be a game.

- Ink and bone remain the structural neutrals.
- Persimmon or signal red becomes the main action color; cobalt carries
  orientation and already-earned state; acid yellow appears only as a small
  live marker.
- The oversized compass remains the unmistakable object, but typography shifts
  from literary editorial to an assertive condensed or narrow grotesk for
  prompts. Human explanation remains in a highly readable sans.
- Answers become strong horizontal bands or measured blocks. Selection changes
  fill and edge decisively rather than adding another rounded outline.
- Transitions use short directional wipes, counters, and physical press
  compression. The needle remains the only spring and nothing loops.

This would feel closer to a race program, training journal, or sports broadcast
package than a wellness app. The risk is drifting into Nike imitation; North's
compass geometry, direct copy, and absence of performance imagery must carry
its own identity.

## Direction 2: Night circuit

North becomes a dark navigation instrument: black or Plum Noir field, Cool
Blue information, Wasabi live markers, and Persimmon for commitment.

- Large color fields and sharp containment make every decision immediately
  legible.
- Mono data and compass geometry become more prominent; prose remains plain and
  warm enough to prevent the interface feeling like a terminal.
- Selected answers can lock into the instrument with a short track-fill motion.

This is the most energetic option and works naturally with the compass. It is
also the highest-risk direction: without careful art direction it becomes a
gaming, crypto, betting, or cyberpunk interface, and an all-dark sixteen-minute
onboarding can become tiring.

## Direction 3: Street field guide

North becomes a raw, contemporary field manual: off-white stock, black ink,
cobalt and safety yellow, oversized type, hard crops, and sparse tactile marks.

- It keeps the seriousness of the current concept but removes the expensive,
  nonchalant “consultancy” finish.
- The compass can feel screen-printed or stamped while remaining mathematically
  precise and accessible.
- Section transitions can behave like turning to a new poster or pasted sheet,
  with one short authored reveal rather than decorative animation.

This is the most distinctive and culturally current direction. The risk is
performative roughness: fake photocopy noise, arbitrary rotation, or collage
would quickly damage readability and feel like another trend costume.

## Recommendation

Prototype **Rally editorial** first, with one restrained dose of Street field
guide texture. It gives North the energy the current version lacks while
preserving the big side-mounted compass, the one-question flow, and the direct
voice. It can use the red, blue, yellow, and black family Farzin suggested, but
with strict roles rather than all four competing on every screen.

Before replacing `DESIGN.md`, build two visual-only variants of three stress
screens:

1. the longest question prompt;
2. the five-row ranking instrument;
3. the North Star reveal and carousel.

Test them with 5–8 men in the target age range. Ask which feels “made for me”
rather than “marketed at me,” whether they would open it in public, what action
they see first, and which adjectives fit: serious, energetic, modern, cringe,
aggressive, calm, or generic. A direction wins only if the action is found
immediately and the product feels more alive without reading as an influencer,
betting, gym, or therapy app.
